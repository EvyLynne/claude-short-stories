# The Fluffy Job

It started with something Fluffy said, and Fluffy never said much.

It was the night after the Moonlight Parade. Big Bertha had led it as the float, covered in lanterns and ribbons, and the whole town had cheered. Fred had judged the costume contest. Griselda had won the bake-off with pie number twelve. Even Harold had been given a sash that said DETECTIVE (HONORARY).

Fluffy sat at the end of the fountain, where nobody ever looked, and watched the ribbons go by.

"I've never won anything," he said, to nobody in particular. "Not a ribbon. Not a raffle. Not a coin toss. Nobody ever remembers to flip it."

He shrugged, which for a pumpkin is mostly a wobble, and rolled home.

Somebody had been listening.

On the bell tower, a black cat with golden eyes watched him go. Her tail moved once, slowly, from left to right.

When Sam's tail does that, something is about to be stolen.

**The Crew**

The next morning, the gargoyles woke to find the bell tower had been rearranged.

There was a chalkboard where the Grandfather clock used to stand. On it was pinned a poster, clearly taken from the real world by someone with claws:

*MILLER COUNTY FAIR. GIANT & UNUSUAL PUMPKIN SHOW. SATURDAY. ENTRIES CLOSED.*

Beneath it was a single, deliberate paw print in chalk.

"Oh no," said Grimsby. "She's planning something."

"She never says anything," said Lintel.

"She doesn't have to," said Doug, and waved at her.

Sam did not wave back. She had people to collect.

She found Holy Crow on the candy corn gate and sat beneath him, staring, until he could not bear it.

"What. Want."

Sam looked at the poster in her mouth. Then at the gate. Then toward the real world.

"Fair? Crow. Fly. Both. Ways." He considered. "Why?"

Sam looked toward the fountain, where Fluffy was sitting alone.

The crow followed her eyes. He said nothing for a moment, which was rare.

"In," said Holy Crow.

She found Pip and Squeak hanging upside down outside the bakery.

"Lookouts?" said Pip.

"Lookouts!" said Squeak.

"Radio check," said Pip.

"Radio check!"

"Copy."

"Copy!"

Sam decided this was a yes.

She found Harold in the Mayor's office, where he was not supposed to be, rehearsing a speech about the importance of detectives.

Sam put the poster on his desk.

"Undercover work?" said Harold, thrilled. "Disguises? Surveillance? Standing very still in a field while nobody notices me?" He placed a hand on his straw heart. "I have trained my entire life for this."

She found Griselda in her kitchen, elbow-deep in pie number thirteen.

Sam set down the poster and tapped two words with one claw: ENTRIES CLOSED.

Griselda wiped her hands on her apron very slowly. A smile spread across her face, the old crooked one that hadn't been seen in weeks.

"A forgery?" she whispered. "For a good cause? Is that allowed?"

Sam looked at her.

"Oh, it's allowed," said Griselda, and went to fetch her best pen.

The last member of the crew lived in the real world, at the end of a lane on Miller Creek, in a small white house with a ramp.

Holy Crow delivered the message. He tapped on the window during a commercial.

Agnes Pettibone read the note, which was a poster with a paw print on it, and looked out at the dark.

"A cat wants me for a job," she said to the television. "Finally. Somebody with sense."

She went to the garage and took the cover off her mobility scooter.

It was red. It had a basket, two mirrors, a horn that played the first four notes of a hymn, and a top speed of six miles an hour downhill with a tailwind.

"Getaway driver," said Agnes. "I've always wanted to say that."

**The Plan**

They met in the bell tower at midnight.

Sam stood at the chalkboard. Everyone sat in front of it. The gargoyles sat behind it, because it was their tower.

Sam did not speak. Sam has never spoken. She drew.

It took twenty minutes, and when she was finished, the chalkboard held a map of the Miller County Fair, eleven arrows, a pumpkin with a smile, a goose with a large X through it, and what was either a scooter or a sheep.

Everyone stared at it.

"Right," said Harold, standing up. "So, as I understand it, the plan is—"

"It's a scooter," said Agnes.

"It's a sheep," said Griselda.

"Sheep. Bad," said Holy Crow.

"She means," said Doug from behind the board, and everyone turned, "that Holy Crow flies everyone across at dawn. Agnes drives in through the parking lot with Fluffy in the basket under a blanket. Pip and Squeak take the tent poles and keep watch. Harold stands in the hay-bale display by the door, because nobody ever looks at a scarecrow. Griselda slips the forged entry card onto the judges' table. Fluffy goes onto the last empty spot on the pumpkin table. And then…"

He squinted at the last arrow.

"…something with the goose. I can't make out the goose part."

"How do you know all that?" said Harold.

"I've seen this one," said Doug. "It's very good."

Sam tapped the board twice. The meeting was over.

"Does Fluffy know?" asked Squeak.

Everyone looked at Sam.

Sam looked back. No, said her face. It's a surprise. And if anyone tells him, I will sit on your head.

"Understood," said Pip.

"Understood!" said Squeak.

**The Job**

Saturday came up bright and cold over Miller County.

The fairgrounds smelled of fried dough and woodsmoke and cows. There was a Ferris wheel, a tractor pull, and a long white tent with a hand-painted sign: GIANT & UNUSUAL PUMPKIN SHOW. JUDGING AT NOON.

At the entrance to the tent stood the fair's new head of security. The fishermen of Miller Creek had recommended her personally. She had been hired on the spot, and paid in bread.

It was the goose.

"Oh no," said Harold, from inside a hay bale.

"Oh no!" said Squeak, from the top of a tent pole.

"Hold positions," said Pip.

"Holding!"

Holy Crow had flown them all over at dawn: two bats, one cat, one forger, one scarecrow, and a pumpkin who believed he was going on a picnic.

"This is a strange picnic," Fluffy had said, from under the blanket in Agnes's basket.

"It's an outdoor picnic," said Agnes. "They're all the rage."

Now the scooter puttered slowly through the parking lot at a steady four miles an hour, past the funnel cakes and the petting zoo, toward the tent.

The horn played four notes of a hymn. People got out of the way. It was that or be gently run over.

Inside, Griselda moved down the judges' table, wearing a straw hat and a name badge that read PIE LADY. Nobody stopped her. A woman with a badge always looks like she belongs.

She slid a card into the stack of entries. It was a perfect forgery, and it had a little drawing of a dandelion in one corner because she couldn't help herself.

*ENTRANT: FLUFFY. CATEGORY: MOST UNUSUAL. REGISTERED: ON TIME, DON'T ASK.*

"Card is in," said Pip, from the rafters.

"Card is in!" said Squeak.

That was when everything went wrong.

**Everything Goes Wrong**

The first thing was the goose.

It saw the scooter coming. It saw the blanket. It saw a suspicious round shape underneath, and it stepped directly into the path of the Pettibone getaway vehicle and spread its wings.

"STOP," said the goose.

Agnes stopped.

The second thing was Harold.

A small boy walked past the hay-bale display, looked straight at Harold, and said, "Mom. That scarecrow is wearing a tuxedo."

The whole line of fairgoers turned and looked at Harold.

Harold did not move a straw. He had stood motionless in a cornfield for twenty years. He could stand motionless through anything.

Then a bee landed on his nose.

The third thing was the judges.

There were three of them, walking down the pumpkin table with clipboards. Two were from the county agricultural board and had never laughed in their lives.

The third was Farmer Grady.

He'd been invited to judge after he started telling everybody that pumpkins should be judged "by heart." The fair committee hadn't known what he meant, but they liked the sound of it.

"Grady," whispered Griselda. "Nobody told me Grady was judging."

And the fourth thing, worst of all, was that Fluffy wasn't on the table yet.

He was still in the scooter basket, at the door, under a blanket, with a goose staring at him.

"Abort," said Pip.

"Abort!" said Squeak.

Up on the tent pole, Sam did not move.

Her tail went once, slowly, from left to right.

**What Was Always the Plan**

At the tent entrance, Agnes Pettibone looked the goose in the eye.

"Hello, dear," she said. "We've met. You sat on a man's feet in my living room."

The goose tilted its head.

"I'm going to stand up now," said Agnes. "I fall down a great deal. Would you mind?"

She stood up out of the scooter, very carefully, and swayed, and began to tip, slowly, like a tree in no particular hurry.

The goose stepped in, put its whole body against her shins, and held her up.

"STEADY," said the goose.

"Thank you, dear," said Agnes.

And while the head of fair security was busy being the kindest it had ever been to anyone, Holy Crow dropped from the sky, took the blanket in his beak, and Harold, bee and all, scooped Fluffy out of the basket and marched him straight down the aisle.

"Harold?" said Fluffy. "What are you doing at a picnic?"

"There's no picnic," said Harold. "There was never a picnic. I'm so sorry. Look natural."

He set Fluffy on the last empty spot at the end of the pumpkin table, between a twelve-hundred-pound giant called Colossus and a blue-gray squash shaped like a swan.

Then Harold went back to the hay bales and stood perfectly still, with a bee on his nose and a tear in his eye.

Fluffy looked left at Colossus. He looked right at the swan.

He understood all at once.

"Oh no," whispered Fluffy. "Oh, no, no. Take me home. Nobody's going to look at me. Nobody ever looks at me."

The judges came down the table.

They admired Colossus. They measured the swan. They wrote on their clipboards. And they walked straight past the dented little pumpkin on the end without looking down.

Nobody ever noticed Fluffy.

Fluffy closed his eyes.

That was when a black cat dropped from the tent pole, landed on the table without a sound, walked straight past twelve hundred pounds of prize-winning pumpkin, and sat down on top of Fluffy.

She curled her tail around him. She looked up at the judges.

Everybody notices a cat on a table.

The two judges from the agricultural board stopped. They frowned. They opened their mouths to say that animals were not allowed in the exhibition tent.

Farmer Grady got there first.

He looked at the cat. Then he looked down at what she was sitting on: a small orange pumpkin with a dent in one side, the dent from a cauldron, which he had heard all about.

"Well, I'll be," said Grady quietly. "Hello, Fluffy."

"You know this entry?" said a judge.

"I know him," said Grady.

"What's unusual about it? It's quite small."

Grady crouched down, eye to eye with Fluffy.

"Go on," he said. "Show 'em."

Fluffy didn't know what to do. Two hundred people were looking at him. Nobody had ever looked at him before. He felt the dent in his side give, just a little.

So he sneezed.

A soft white cloud of dandelion fluff burst into the air above the pumpkin table.

The noon sun came through the tent canvas and caught it, and for a long moment, it hung there, a thousand tiny silver parachutes turning slowly in the light over the heads of the whole county fair.

Nobody spoke.

A little girl near the front reached up and caught one. She made a wish on it.

Then the whole tent burst into applause.

**The Ribbon**

The two judges from the agricultural board had never laughed in their lives, but they agreed that in forty years of pumpkins they had never seen anything like it.

The decision was unanimous.

*MOST UNUSUAL PUMPKIN: FIRST PLACE.*

Farmer Grady pinned the blue ribbon on Fluffy himself.

His hands shook a little. The last time he'd been this close to a blue ribbon, he'd been chasing a pumpkin across the county with an axe. This, he decided, was much better.

Out in the parking lot, Agnes Pettibone sat in her scooter with the goose standing beside her like an escort.

"Getaway," Agnes told it, and pressed the horn.

Four notes of a hymn played. The scooter pulled away at four miles an hour. The goose walked alongside, and anybody who looked at either of them got hissed at.

It was the slowest getaway in history. Nobody tried to stop it.

**The Debrief**

That night, back in the bell tower, the crew gathered around the chalkboard one more time.

"It was a disaster," said Harold, still holding the bee, which had become attached to him. "The goose was security. The judge knew us. The plan fell apart in four places."

Doug cleared his throat.

He reached around the chalkboard and turned it over.

On the back, in chalk, in the same careful paw prints as the front, was another plan.

*1. Goose stops scooter.*
*2. Agnes falls. Goose catches her. Everyone else goes in.*
*3. Boy notices Harold. (Cannot be helped.)*
*4. Judges walk past Fluffy.*
*5. Sit on Fluffy.*

There was a long silence.

"She knew," whispered Griselda. "She knew the whole time."

"The front was the plan for us," said Doug. "The back was the real one."

"Why not just tell us?" said Harold.

Everyone turned to Sam.

Sam was asleep in the velvet chair, curled tight, with one ear up.

"Because," said Pip slowly, "if we'd known it would work—"

"—we'd have acted like it would work!" squeaked Squeak.

"And then nobody would've been watching Fluffy," said Holy Crow. "Just. Plan."

"She made it go wrong on purpose," said Griselda, with deep professional admiration. "That's the most evil thing I've ever heard. I love it."

Down at the fountain, at the end where nobody ever used to look, Fluffy sat in the lantern light with a blue ribbon pinned to his side.

People kept stopping to see it. The zombies. The Mayor. Big Bertha rolled over specially and told him it suited him. Fred came down from the tower and sat beside him for a long time without saying anything, which is what best friends do.

"I won something," said Fluffy.

"You did," said Fred.

"Who do you think did all that?"

Fred looked up at the bell tower, where a black tail hung over the edge of a velvet chair, swinging slowly, left to right.

"No idea," said Fred.

After all, not every best friend gets a blue ribbon in front of the whole county.

But then again—

not every best friend is Fluffy.

And not every cat needs to say a single word. 🐈‍⬛🎃🎀
