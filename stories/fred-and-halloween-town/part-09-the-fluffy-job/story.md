# The Fluffy Job

*Written by Evelyn, with assistance from Claude (AI)*

*Fred and Halloween Town, Part 9. Version 3 of 3: see the [prompt log](prompt-log.md) for how it was made.*

Fluffy had never won anything.

Not a ribbon. Not a raffle. Not a coin toss. Nobody ever remembered to flip the coin.

“Just once,” he sighed, watching Big Bertha roll by in her parade ribbons, “I’d like to win something.”

Up on the bell tower, a black cat with golden eyes heard him.

Her tail flicked once, left to right.

When Sam’s tail does that, something is about to be stolen.

Sam had a problem. Sam does not talk. So she put her crew together the only way she knew how: she stared at people until they agreed.

She stared at Harold until he said, “Yes, all right, whatever it is.”

She stared at Pip and Squeak until they said, “We’re in!” and “We’re in!”

Then Holy Crow flew a note across to the real world, to a small white house on Miller Creek. The note was a paw print on a poster for the county fair.

Agnes Pettibone read it, took the cover off her mobility scooter, and said, “Finally. A job.”

That night, Sam explained the plan.

She drew it on a chalkboard. It took her twenty minutes. When she finished, the board showed a big circle, a lot of arrows, a scribbled cloud, and the county fair.

The crew squinted at it.

“That’s a pumpkin,” said Agnes.

“That’s a sheep,” said Harold.

“Sheep!” said Pip.

“Sheep!” said Squeak.

Sam tapped the circle. It was obviously Fluffy. The cloud was obviously a ribbon.

“Definitely a sheep,” said Harold. “Look, it’s fluffy.”

Three against one. Sam put down the chalk and went to bed.

So that was the plan: get Fluffy into the county fair, disguised as a sheep.

Nobody told Fluffy. Sam would have, if she talked.

Saturday morning, Fluffy woke up covered in cotton balls.

“Why am I covered in cotton balls?” said Fluffy.

“You’re a sheep,” said Harold.

“I’m a pumpkin.”

“Not today.”

Agnes loaded him into the scooter basket. Harold climbed on the back. Pip and Squeak hung from the mirrors. Sam rode on top, looking like a cat who had been outvoted by idiots.

The scooter went four miles an hour. Its horn played the first four notes of “Amazing Grace.”

Agnes had it put in after the church parking lot incident. An ordinary horn, she said, made people angry. “Amazing Grace” made them step aside and bow their heads, and some of them crossed themselves on the way. She had never once had to wait at a crosswalk.

*A-maz-ing grace,* honked the scooter, all the way down the county road.

Three people stepped off the shoulder. A farmer took off his hat. They got to the fairground by lunch.

Sam pointed a paw firmly at the big white tent marked PUMPKIN SHOW.

Harold steered them firmly into the barn marked SHEEP SHOW.

The sheep looked at Fluffy.

Fluffy looked at the sheep.

“Baa?” said Fluffy.

The sheep did not buy it.

“Nobody panic,” said Harold. “Act natural.”

Pip and Squeak took up their lookout posts in the rafters, which went well until a judge walked in.

“LOOKOUT!” shouted Pip.

“LOOKOUT!” shouted Squeak.

Every sheep in the barn panicked at once. So did two goats who weren’t even in the competition.

When the dust settled, the judge was standing in the middle of the ring with a clipboard, a hat full of hay, and a small round sheep made of cotton balls at his feet.

It was Farmer Grady.

He looked down at the sheep. The sheep had a green leaf sticking out of its top.

He looked over at Harold, who was standing very still in the corner in his tuxedo, pretending to be a scarecrow.

Harold had a sheep on his foot.

“Harold,” said Grady.

“I’m not here,” said Harold.

Grady crouched down.

“Hello, Fluffy,” he said quietly.

Fluffy panicked.

Two hundred people were looking at him. The sheep were looking at him. Grady was looking at him. In his whole life, nobody had ever looked at Fluffy.

His dent popped, and he sneezed.

A great white cloud of dandelion fluff burst out of him and drifted over the whole barn. It settled on the sheep, on the judges, and on the crowd. Some of it landed in Agnes’s hair, and she decided it suited her.

For a moment, everything in the barn was soft and white and floating.

Nobody had ever seen a sheep do that.

The other two judges stared at their clipboards. They had been judging sheep for thirty years. They had no category for this.

Grady pulled a blue ribbon from his pocket.

“Most Unusual Sheep,” he announced. “First place.”

The crowd went wild. The goats went wild. One sheep fainted.

On the ride home, nobody said much.

Fluffy sat in the scooter basket wearing his blue ribbon and three remaining cotton balls.

“I won something,” he whispered.

“You won Best Sheep,” said Harold.

“I’ll take it.”

“Take it!” said Pip and Squeak.

Sam rode on top of the scooter with her back to all of them.

Back in Halloween Town, Fluffy would not take off the cotton balls.

He wore them to breakfast. He wore them to the fountain. He wore them to bed, with the ribbon on top. When anyone said, “Good morning, Fluffy,” he answered, “Baa,” with great dignity.

On Monday, Holy Crow dropped a letter on the fountain.

“Fair! Letter! Fancy!”

It was from the Miller County Fair. Mr. Fluffy, Most Unusual Sheep, was invited to compete at the State Sheep Championship. Travel paid. Bring your own hooves.

Fluffy glowed.

Holy Crow dropped a second letter.

It was from a wool company. They had heard about the dandelion fluff, and they wanted to know how soon he could be sheared.

“Sheared?” said Fluffy.

The cotton balls were off by lunch.

On Tuesday, eleven sheep turned up at the candy corn gate with a petition.

Harold read it out to the square.

“‘To whom it may concern. That is not a sheep. That is a pumpkin. We would like our ribbon back.’ Signed, with hoofprints.”

“Lies,” said Fluffy.

The sheep did not move. Neither did Fluffy.

Then Sam walked down from the bell tower, sat in front of the gate, and stared at the sheep.

She did not blink.

One by one, the sheep remembered they had somewhere else to be.

That evening, the crew gathered by the fountain while Fluffy polished his ribbon.

“Well,” said Harold, leaning back with great satisfaction. “Another flawless plan.”

Sam turned her head and looked at him.

She looked at him for a very long time.

Harold went home early.

After all, not every best friend wins a blue ribbon at the county fair.

But then again—

not every best friend is Fluffy.

And not every sheep is a sheep. 🐈‍⬛🎃🐑
