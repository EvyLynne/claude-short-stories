# Fred and the Scarecrow Curse

Farmer Grady had not slept well in a year.

Every night he dreamed of one small, smiling pumpkin rolling away.

So one foggy October evening, he marched into the woods to find him.

He brought Harold, his scarecrow, slung over one shoulder. Grady told himself it was for company.

He followed a crooked sign.

He passed through a gate made of candy corn.

And something in the shadows of Halloween Town opened one red eye.

It was a demon. A small one. Its name was Grudge.

Grudge lived wherever nobody looked: under the bakery stairs, behind the post office, inside the mayor's spare top hat.

Grudge ate resentment.

And Farmer Grady was a buffet.

"A whole year of grumbling," the demon whispered. "Delicious."

It snapped its fingers.

Grady's left thumb turned to straw.

He stared at it.

Then his elbow went stiff. His boots filled with hay. Harold's burlap arm wouldn't let go of his shoulder.

They were sticking together.

"No," said Grady. "No, no, no."

He pulled. Harold pulled back, though not on purpose.

He ran for the gate. His knees bent the wrong way, like a scarecrow's in the wind.

He grabbed a lamppost and held on. Straw poured out of his sleeves.

"HELP!" he shouted, and a crow landed on his hat.

High above, two bats spotted him.

"Human in distress, town square," reported Pip.

"Town square!" squeaked Squeak.

They flew straight to the Grand Pumpkin Tower.

Princess Fred was back from vacation, and he rolled down at once, with Sam padding beside him and Fluffy grumbling behind.

Fred stopped.

He knew that hat. He knew that beard.

"Farmer Grady?"

Grady looked down. His face was half skin, half burlap.

"You," he croaked.

His nose turned into a carrot stub.

"Every time he gets angry, it gets worse," said Fluffy. "I'm not a doctor, but that seems important."

The Mayor arrived, all twelve feet of him, and studied the farmer closely.

"This is not how scarecrows are made," he said. "We are stuffed with care. He is being stuffed with spite."

Sam's fur stood on end.

Cats can see things others can't.

She stared at the shadow under the lamppost and growled.

"Demon," said Fred. "Pip, Squeak, find it!"

The bats swept the square, squeaking into every dark corner.

"Echo off something small and rotten, behind the fountain," said Pip.

"Rotten!" squeaked Squeak.

Sam pounced.

Grudge shot out of the shadows, hissing, twice the size it had been an hour ago.

"You can't stop it," the demon sneered. "He hates that pumpkin. He'll be mine by midnight."

Grady fought.

He clenched the one fist he had left. He dug his real heel into the cobblestones. He pulled against the straw with everything a stubborn old farmer has.

It wasn't enough.

Fred rolled right up to his boots.

"Farmer Grady," he said, "I'm not angry at you."

The farmer blinked.

"You wanted to make me into pie."

"I did," Grady whispered.

"And I got a kingdom out of it. So thank you, in a way."

Grady's burlap chin trembled.

"I measured you every morning," he said. "I never once looked at you."

He turned his stiff neck toward the scarecrow fused to his shoulder.

"And Harold. I hit you with an axe. I'm sorry."

Grudge shrieked.

It shrank to the size of a cat. Then a chicken. Then a candy corn.

The straw fell out of Grady's sleeves. His thumb turned pink. His knees remembered which way to bend.

Harold dropped free and landed on his own two posts, blinking his button eyes.

Sam swatted the tiny demon across the square.

It landed with a plop in a steaming mug held by Griselda Wartwhistle, who was working the free pumpkin spice stand.

She put a lid on it.

"I'll keep an eye on that," she said. "I know trouble when I've been it."

Farmer Grady sat on the edge of the fountain, entirely human and entirely tired.

The Mayor offered Harold a job, and a tuxedo.

Harold accepted both.

Fred escorted the farmer to the candy corn gate himself.

"Come back for the Moonlight Parade," he said. "You can judge the pumpkins."

"By size?" asked Grady.

Fred smiled his familiar smile.

"By heart."

Grady walked home lighter than he'd come, with one piece of straw still stuck in his hat.

He left it there.

After all, not every little pumpkin can forgive the farmer who chased him with an axe.

But then again—

not every little pumpkin is Fred. 🎃🌾
