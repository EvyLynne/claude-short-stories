# Pumpkins in the Wild

*Written by Evelyn, with assistance from Claude (AI)*

*Fred and Halloween Town, Part 11. Version 4 of 4: see the [prompt log](prompt-log.md) for how it was made.*

The film crew arrived on a Monday in a muddy green truck.

There was Dee Rector, the director, who had a clipboard and had not slept since 2019. There was Leonardo Lense, the cameraman, who had once spent eleven weeks in a hole in Antarctica waiting for a penguin to sneeze. And there was Mike Boomer Rang, who held a microphone on a long pole. Whenever he swung it, it came back around and hit him.

And there was Sir Ambrose Thistlewick, the most famous nature narrator in the world.

Sir Ambrose had narrated whales and volcanoes. He had once narrated a snail crossing a garden path, and people wept in cinemas from Boston to Brisbane. He whispered everything, slowly, and he took his time over names.

“Deeee… rector,” he breathed, holding up a clipping from the Miller County Gazette. “Look.”

LOCAL PUMPKIN SEEN ROLLING. FISHERMEN SAY “IT’S HAPPENED BEFORE.”

“The wild pumpkin,” whispered Sir Ambrose. “Nobody has ever filmed one.”

They followed a crooked sign through the woods and walked through a gate made of candy corn.

Up on the bell tower, the gargoyles saw them first.

“Humans,” said Grimsby. “Four of them. Carrying equipment.”

“Tax collectors,” said Lintel. “They’ve come to tax the fog.”

“They’re whispering,” said Doug. “Nobody whispers about taxes. They shout about taxes.”

“Then they’re bakers,” said Lintel. “Look at the big one with the clipboard. That’s a recipe. They’ve heard about the pumpkins, and they’ve brought a pie list.”

Grimsby squinted. “The long pole with the fuzzy end. What’s that for?”

“Dusting,” said Doug. “They’re very clean bakers.”

“And the big black thing with the barrel?”

The three gargoyles stared at it for a long moment.

“That,” said Lintel, “is a cannon.”

“Last time humans brought a cannon,” said Grimsby darkly, “it was 1782, and my cousin lost his nose.”

“Your cousin invented the moon,” said Doug.

“Different cousin.”

They took a deep breath, all three of them, and shouted it to the whole town.

“HUMANS!” bellowed Grimsby. “THREE HUMANS AND A WHISPERER!”

“THEY HAVE A CANNON!” shouted Lintel. “AND A PIE LIST!”

“IT MIGHT BE A CAMERA!” shouted Doug. “EVERYBODY ACT NATURAL! NOBODY SHOW OFF! ESPECIALLY YOU, HAROLD!”

Nobody listened.

Fred was sitting on top of the Grand Pumpkin Tower when he looked down and saw something enormous and black pointed straight at him from the bushes.

It had a long barrel. It had a lid. It swiveled when he moved.

“They’ve brought a cannon,” whispered Fred. “Someone has declared war on a pumpkin.”

He ducked behind his crown, which did not hide very much of him.

“And here,” whispered Sir Ambrose, “high on his rocky perch, the wild pumpkin. Notice the crown, which he has almost certainly stolen from a smaller animal.”

“I didn’t steal it!” Fred called down. “I was crowned!”

The crew didn’t hear a word. Nature narrators never hear the animals.

Since the cannon wasn’t firing, Fred decided to act natural. He had no idea how a wild pumpkin acted, so he sat perfectly still.

He sat still for two hours.

“He is playing dead,” whispered Sir Ambrose. “A classic defense.”

At the end of the second hour, Fred’s leaf itched. He wiggled it.

“MOVEMENT!” hissed Dee.

“Did you get it, Leonardo?” whispered Sir Ambrose.

“I got it,” whispered Leonardo, with tears in his eyes.

Mike Boomer Rang swung the microphone in for a closer listen. It came back around and hit him.

By lunch, all of Halloween Town knew about the camera, and nobody acted natural again.

The vampire posed on his balcony in his best cape and immediately regretted it, because it was noon. He began to smoke gently around the edges. He finished his close-up under a beach umbrella the zombies lent him.

The zombies shuffled past the camera, then ran around the block to shuffle past again.

Big Bertha parked directly in front of the lens.

“The great matriarch,” whispered Sir Ambrose, “guarding her young.”

Bertha turned slightly to the left to show her good side.

Harold stood in the square in his tuxedo, arms out, in a heroic pose, all afternoon.

“A scarecrow,” whispered Sir Ambrose. “Introduced by farmers. Entirely harmless.”

Harold was offended for a week.

Pip and Squeak did loops around the bell tower.

“The bats are echolocating,” whispered Sir Ambrose. “They send out a call, and it bounces back.”

“Hello!” called Pip.

“Hello!” called Squeak.

“Nature is perfect,” whispered Sir Ambrose.

“I’m not an echo,” Squeak muttered to Pip. “I’m a whole bat.”

“A whole bat!” said Pip, before he could stop himself.

A ghost floated right up to the lens and waved for a full minute.

Ghosts do not show up on film. When Leonardo played the footage back, it showed an empty street and a ham sandwich floating along by itself at head height.

“A rare airborne sandwich,” whispered Sir Ambrose. “Deeee… rector. We are making history.”

Then Leonardo tried to film Sam.

Every time he pointed the camera at her, she stood up and walked out of the shot. She didn’t hurry. She just left.

Leonardo hid a camera in a log with a saucer of cream in front of it and lay in the bushes all night.

In the morning, the cream was gone. On the lens was a single paw print.

“I have filmed the snow leopard,” whispered Sir Ambrose, shaken. “I have never seen anything like her.”

On the third day, the goose arrived with Farmer Grady.

It saw three strangers in its bushes.

“WHO,” said the goose.

“Ah, the Canada goose,” whispered Sir Ambrose. “Notice the gentle—”

“YOU,” said the goose.

Leonardo kept filming. You never stop filming.

He filmed the goose biting the microphone. Mike Boomer Rang swung the pole to shake it off, and it came back around with the goose still on it and hit him.

He filmed Dee running into the bakery. He filmed Sir Ambrose being chased twice around the fountain, still whispering.

“Highly territorial—”

“MINE.”

“—do not approach—”

“MINE.”

They spent the rest of the day up a tree.

At sunset, the crew set up on the hill for the final shot.

Sir Ambrose cleared his throat to speak the closing words, and felt something watching him.

He turned around.

Four hundred citizens of Halloween Town were in the bushes behind him, filming him back. The zombies had binoculars. The Mayor had Agnes Pettibone’s old video camera, which still had her 1987 Christmas on it.

The crew couldn’t hear a word, but on the town’s tape, Pip and Squeak were whispering the narration.

“And here,” whispered Pip, “the rare documentary crew.”

“The rare documentary crew!” whispered Squeak.

“Far from their natural habitat,” whispered Pip. “Notice the enormous camera. They are about to flee.”

They fled.

*Pumpkins in the Wild* aired in the spring and was nominated for every nature award there is.

Fred watched the awards ceremony on channel nine at Agnes Pettibone’s house, if you jiggle the antenna.

He had been nominated for Best Performance by a Wild Animal.

So had the airborne sandwich.

So had the goose.

“And the winner is…” said the presenter, opening the envelope.

Fred held his breath. He had sat still for two whole hours for that film.

“…the goose.”

The goose was there in person. Farmer Grady had driven it to the ceremony in the wheelbarrow, wearing a bow tie.

It walked onto the stage. It looked at the trophy. It looked at the presenter.

It bit the presenter.

The audience gave it a standing ovation.

Sir Ambrose stepped up to the microphone to accept on the goose’s behalf. He leaned in close and whispered, “Deeee… rector, are we still rolling?”

Mike Boomer Rang swung the microphone in for the speech.

It came back around and hit Sir Ambrose.

The goose bit him too.

Up on the bell tower, the gargoyles watched the ceremony through the window of the bakery across the square.

“Told you it was a camera,” said Doug.

“It was a cannon,” said Lintel. “Look what it did to them.”

Back at the little white house on Miller Creek, Fred stared at the television.

“I sat still for two hours,” he said. “Two hours. And I lost to a goose.”

Agnes Pettibone patted his leaf.

“Should’ve bitten somebody, dear,” she said. “Nobody gives awards for sitting still.”

After all, not every little pumpkin loses Best Wild Animal to a goose.

But then again—

not every little pumpkin is Fred.

And not every wild animal is the wildest one on screen. 🎃🎥🪿
